How to Throw a Vase

A classic vase — wide belly, narrowed neck, flared rim — is one of the most satisfying forms to throw. It builds on fundamental cylinder technique and introduces collaring, a key skill for any enclosed or necked form. Follow each step in order and work slowly; the wheel rewards patience.

Wedged clay ball

Step 1

Wedge Your Clay

Take 1.5 lbs of stoneware and wedge it for 2–3 minutes on a flat surface using the ram's head method — push forward and down with the heel of your hand, rotate a quarter turn, repeat. You're driving out air bubbles and making the clay uniform throughout.

Tip: Slice the ball in half with your wire cutter. No air holes or streaks = ready to throw. If you see any, keep wedging.

Centering

Step 2

Center the Clay

Slam the clay firmly onto the center of the wheel head. Set the wheel to medium-fast. Wet your hands, brace your elbows against your thighs, and use the heel of your left hand to push inward while your right hand pushes down. Work the clay into a smooth, wobble-free cone.

Tip: Rest a finger lightly against the spinning clay — if it bounces, it's not centered. The clay should feel almost "not there."

Opening the floor

Step 3

Open the Clay

With the wheel at medium speed, press both thumbs into the very center of the clay and push straight down — stopping about ½ inch from the wheel head. That's your floor. Pull your fingers outward to widen the base to the diameter you want inside the vase bottom.

Tip: Compress the floor in circular motions with your fingertips. A firm, compressed floor prevents cracking in the kiln.

Pulling up walls

Step 4

Pull Up the Walls into a Cylinder

Place fingers inside and outside the wall simultaneously. Squeeze gently and pull upward in one smooth, continuous motion from the base to the rim. Add a little water each pull. Repeat 3–4 times, thinning the walls and gaining height. Aim for a tall, even cylinder — this is the foundation of your vase.

Tip: Always start each pull from the very bottom. Starting mid-wall creates a thick base and thin top. Let the inside hand lead slightly.

Shaping the belly

Step 5

Shape the Belly

Slow the wheel to medium. Place your dominant hand inside the cylinder, fingers pointing outward. Support with your outside hand. Apply gentle outward pressure starting at the lower third of the wall and move upward — pushing the clay into a wide, round belly. This is called "bellying out."

Tip: Work gradually over 2–3 passes. Keep the top quarter of the wall straight or slightly inward — you'll collar that into the neck next. Don't over-widen or the walls will collapse.

Collaring the neck

Step 6

Collar the Neck

Slow the wheel right down. Wrap both hands around the upper portion of the cylinder — fingers pointing upward — and gently squeeze inward and slightly upward simultaneously. Move slowly and evenly. The neck will narrow and rise. Repeat 2–3 times to reach your desired neck width.

Tip: Never collar too much in one pass — the clay will buckle. Small increments work best. A neck about ⅓ the width of the belly looks naturally proportional.

Flaring the rim

Step 7

Open & Flare the Rim

With one finger inside the neck and one supporting the outside, gently push outward and slightly downward to flare the rim into a soft trumpet shape. Use very light pressure — the neck walls are thin. A flared rim softens the silhouette and gives the vase a finished, elegant look.

Tip: Wet your fingers well. Finish by running a damp chamois or sponge along the rim edge to compress and smooth it — this prevents cracking as it dries.

Wire off the wheel

Step 8

Wire Off & Set to Dry

Stop the wheel completely. Hold your wire cutter taut and flat against the wheel head, then pull straight toward you in one smooth motion. Slide the vase onto a bat or board and place it in a cool, draft-free spot. It needs to reach "leather hard" — firm but not fully dry — before trimming.

Tip: Cover the vase loosely with plastic to slow drying evenly. A narrow neck dries faster than the belly and can crack if exposed to air on one side.

Trimming the foot

Step 9

Trim the Foot Ring

Once leather hard, center the vase upside down on the wheel. Secure it with three small coils of soft clay, or use a chuck (a thrown cylinder) to hold it by the neck. With the wheel spinning, use a loop tool to carve a foot ring into the base — a raised circular rim that lifts the piece and gives it a refined, professional finish.

Tip: Tap the inverted vase — a solid sound means it's ready; a hollow thud means too dry; a very dull thud means too wet. Aim for slightly firm but still cool to the touch.

Finished & glazed

Step 10

Bisque Fire, Glaze & Final Fire

Allow the vase to dry completely (1–2 weeks depending on thickness). Bisque fire to ~1800°F. Once cooled, wax the foot ring and dip the vase into glaze for 3–4 seconds for an even coat. Leave the foot ring clean. Load into the kiln for the final glaze firing, unload after cooling, and admire the result.

Tip: A teal or celadon glaze beautifully shows off the curves of a vase. Try different glazes inside and out — they often interact beautifully where they meet at the rim.
